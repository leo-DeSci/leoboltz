# Product Narrative

Build a clean, editorial-style portfolio website for a product manager. The design should feel like a well-crafted online publication — text-forward, highly readable, with excellent typography and intentional use of white space. Think closer to a thoughtful blog or digital magazine than a typical tech portfolio.

STRUCTURE & PAGES:

Homepage:

- Hero section with name, headline, and positioning statement

- Featured case study card (large, prominent)

- Work experience timeline section

- Education section

- Simple footer with contact CTA

Case Study Detail Page:

- Dedicated page for the featured case study

- Clean reading experience with clear hierarchy

- Space for images, pull quotes, and structured sections

- Back navigation to homepage

DESIGN DIRECTION:

- Editorial and text-forward aesthetic

- Serif font for headings (something elegant and readable like Lora, Crimson Text, or similar)

- Sans-serif for body text (clean, modern like Inter or similar)

- Generous line-height and comfortable reading width (max ~700px for body text)

- Subtle use of color — not corporate pastel, something with more character

- High contrast for readability

- Responsive and mobile-friendly

- No generic stock photos or decorative illustrations — let typography and layout do the work

CONTENT TO INCLUDE:

Hero Section:

Name: [Your Name]

Headline: "Building products for the age of agents — and doing the discovery work to make sure they solve real problems."

Positioning statement: "I'm a product manager who leads with discovery — user research, creative problem-solving, and the kind of empathy that catches what people don't say out loud. I'm most useful when the problem isn't fully defined yet, and I'm currently building toward AI-native product work where figuring out what to build is harder than building it. I do my best work at early-to-growth stage companies where product decisions still have real consequence."

Featured Case Study (card on homepage):

Title: "[Case Study Title - Placeholder]"

Summary: "A brief 1-2 sentence summary of what this case study is about and why it matters."

CTA: "Read the full case study" → links to dedicated case study page

Work Experience Section:

- Timeline or list format

- Each entry should have: Company name, role title, dates, 2-3 bullet points of key responsibilities or achievements

- Include placeholder entries for 2-3 previous roles

- Clean, scannable format

Education Section:

- Degree, institution, graduation year

- Include placeholder for 1-2 educational entries

- Keep it simple and uncluttered

Footer/Contact:

- Simple CTA like "Let's talk" or "Get in touch"

- Email link or contact method

- Optional: LinkedIn link

- No social media icon spam

Case Study Detail Page:

- Full case study with these sections as placeholders:

  * The Situation (context and problem)

  * What I Found (discovery and insight)

  * What I Built (solution and approach)

  * What Happened (outcomes and learnings)

  * What I'd Do Differently (reflection)

- Each section should have clear heading hierarchy

- Space for inserting images or diagrams later

- Comfortable reading experience

INTERACTION & NAVIGATION:

- Simple top navigation: logo/name on left, "Work" and "Contact" links on right

- Smooth scrolling where appropriate

- Case study card should be clearly clickable

- Back button on case study detail page to return home

- Clean hover states on interactive elements

OVERALL VIBE:

This should feel confident, mature, and considered. Not flashy or gimmicky. The design should make the content easy to read and the person's thinking easy to follow. It should signal craft and attention to detail without showing off. Think: "This person takes their work seriously and knows how to communicate clearly."

Make it feel like a portfolio that could belong to someone transitioning from junior to mid-level PM — polished enough to signal professional maturity, but honest and direct rather than trying to look more senior than they are.
